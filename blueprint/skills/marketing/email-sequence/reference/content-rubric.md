# Content Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate every piece of marketing content before delivery. Score each dimension 1-10. Apply the per-content-type weight adjustments at the bottom.

**Pass threshold: average score >= 7, no individual score below 5.**

---

## Dimension 1: Clarity

*Is the core message immediately obvious? Could a reader summarize the point in one sentence after reading?*

**1-3 (Poor):**
The reader finishes and cannot articulate what the piece was about. Multiple competing messages. Jargon without explanation. The writer tried to say everything and ended up saying nothing. Reader would need to re-read to extract the point.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
The core message is present but buried. Reader can find it if they look, but it is not obvious on first read. Some competing messages or tangents that dilute the main point. The piece would benefit from a clearer thesis statement or through-line.

**7-8 (Good):**
Core message is clear within the first few sentences. Every paragraph supports or advances that message. Reader could summarize the point in one sentence. No significant tangents or competing messages.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
The core message is unmistakable from the first line. Every sentence serves the message. No waste. The piece has a clear arc: setup, development, payoff. Even a distracted, skimming reader would get the point. The message is specific — not "marketing is important" but "measuring onboarding by completion rate hides your churn problem."

---

## Dimension 2: Hook Strength

*Would the target audience keep reading after the first line? Does the opening create enough tension, curiosity, or value to earn attention?*

**1-3 (Poor):**
Opens with a generic statement, definition, or cliche. "In today's digital landscape..." or "Marketing is an important part of any business." The opening could be swapped onto any article about any topic. No reason for the target audience to continue reading. Zero tension or curiosity.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
The opening is relevant to the topic and audience but predictable. A question that the reader already knows the answer to. A statistic without surprise. The reader might continue but would not feel compelled to. The hook works but does not grab.

**7-8 (Good):**
The opening targets a specific audience and creates a reason to read on. Uses a surprising data point, a counterintuitive claim, a specific story, or a direct challenge. The reader feels "this is for me" and "I want to know more." The hook could not be swapped onto a different article.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
The opening is impossible to ignore. It creates an information gap that demands closure, challenges a deeply held assumption, or presents a scenario so specific the reader feels personally called out. The audience would screenshot it and share it. It works even if the reader never finishes the rest.

---

## Dimension 3: Specificity

*Are there concrete details, numbers, examples, and evidence? Or is the content filled with vague generalities that sound good but say nothing?*

**1-3 (Poor):**
Entirely abstract. "Companies should leverage data-driven strategies to optimize their processes." No numbers, no named examples, no concrete scenarios. Every sentence could mean anything. The reader learns nothing actionable. Reads like a summary of summaries.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
Some specific elements mixed with generalities. A few numbers or examples present but not enough to build credibility. Claims are made but not always supported. The reader gets some value but is left wanting proof for the key assertions.

**7-8 (Good):**
Most claims are supported with specifics. Numbers are used to quantify impact. Named examples or case studies illustrate key points. The reader could take action based on the information provided. Concrete enough to be useful, not just interesting.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
Every major claim has evidence. Numbers are precise, not rounded. Examples are real and named, not hypothetical. The piece includes details that the reader could not find by Googling for 5 minutes — original data, specific case studies, exact processes. The content is a primary source, not a secondary one.

---

## Dimension 4: Voice Authenticity

*Does the content sound like a knowledgeable human wrote it? Or does it read like AI output, corporate boilerplate, or a first-year marketing student's essay?*

**1-3 (Poor):**
Reads obviously machine-generated. Riddled with phrases from the taboo list ("leverage," "cutting-edge," "in today's landscape"). Sentences are uniform length. No personality, no perspective, no rough edges. Could have been written by any AI for any company about any topic. Fails the taboo phrase scan completely.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
Mostly avoids the worst AI cliches but still feels templated. The voice is professional but generic. No strong personality or perspective. The writer plays it safe, presenting "both sides" without committing to a view. A few taboo phrases remain. Sentence rhythm is somewhat varied but still predictable.

**7-8 (Good):**
Sounds like a real person with expertise. Has a point of view and commits to it. Sentence length varies naturally. Uses plain language for complex ideas. No taboo phrases detected. Includes at least one moment of genuine insight or opinion that could not come from a template.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
Unmistakably human. Has a distinct voice the reader would recognize in another piece. Includes moments of wit, self-awareness, or vulnerability that AI cannot replicate. The writer clearly has real experience with the subject. Rhythm is musical — short sentences for impact, longer ones for nuance. You could read a paragraph and know who wrote it.

---

## Dimension 5: CTA Effectiveness

*Is the desired next action clear, compelling, and appropriate to the content and audience?*

**1-3 (Poor):**
No CTA at all, or the CTA is buried, vague, or disconnected from the content. "Contact us to learn more" with no reason given. The CTA asks for too much too soon (e.g., "Buy now" at the end of an awareness-stage blog post). Or the piece has 3+ competing CTAs that confuse the reader.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
A CTA is present and relevant but not compelling. "Sign up for our newsletter" without a reason. The CTA is logically connected to the content but does not create urgency or desire. The reader understands what to do but has no emotional motivation to do it.

**7-8 (Good):**
One clear CTA that is a natural extension of the content. The reader has been given enough value and evidence that the CTA feels like a logical next step, not an ask. The CTA is specific (not "learn more" but "download the onboarding checklist" or "book a 15-minute audit"). Low friction relative to the commitment level.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
The CTA feels inevitable. The content has built such a strong case that NOT taking the action would feel like leaving value on the table. The CTA is specific, low-friction, and time-sensitive without being manipulative. It matches the reader's awareness level perfectly. The reader feels like the CTA is a favor to them, not a request from the writer.

---

## Dimension 6: SEO Alignment

*Does the content serve search intent and follow technical SEO best practices? (Score N/A if SEO is not relevant, e.g., internal emails.)*

**1-3 (Poor):**
No keyword targeting. No heading structure. No internal or external links. Title tag missing or over 70 characters. Meta description absent or duplicated from body text. The content will not rank because search engines cannot understand its purpose or relevance.

**4-6 (Adequate):**
Primary keyword is present but not optimized in placement. Heading structure exists but has issues (multiple H1s, skipped levels, no keywords in H2s). Some internal links but with poor anchor text. Title tag and meta description exist but are not optimized for length or click-through rate. The content could rank with work.

**7-8 (Good):**
Primary keyword appears in title, H1, first 100 words, and naturally throughout at 0.5-2.5% density. Secondary keywords are present. Clean heading hierarchy. 3-5 internal links with descriptive anchors. 2-3 external links to authoritative sources. Title tag is 50-60 characters with keyword in first 4 words. Meta description is 150-160 characters with a CTA verb. Appropriate schema markup recommended.

**9-10 (Excellent):**
All criteria from 7-8, plus: content length meets or exceeds competitor average. Content covers subtopics that competitors miss. FAQ section targets additional long-tail keywords. Image alt text is optimized. URL slug is clean and keyword-rich. The content is genuinely the best result for the target query — it would earn the click and satisfy the searcher even without any link building.

## How to Use Weights

1. Score each dimension 1-10 using the raw criteria above
2. Multiply each raw score by the weight for the content type
3. Average the weighted scores
4. Pass threshold remains: weighted average >= 7, no raw score below 5
5. If any raw dimension scores below 5, it must be fixed regardless of weighting
