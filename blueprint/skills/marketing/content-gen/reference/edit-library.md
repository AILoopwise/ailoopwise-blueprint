# Edit Library: Before/After Transformations

Use this library when humanizing content. Each entry shows an AI-written passage, its human rewrite, and why the change works. Apply these patterns whenever you encounter similar constructions.

---

## Openers

### Edit 1: Generic Landscape Opener

**Category:** Opener
**Before:**
"In today's rapidly evolving digital marketing landscape, businesses must adapt their strategies to stay competitive. The rise of AI-powered tools has created unprecedented opportunities for marketers to optimize their campaigns and deliver personalized experiences at scale."

**After:**
"Most marketing teams run the same playbook they used in 2019. The tools changed. The approach did not. That gap is where budget goes to die."

**Why it works:** The original says nothing specific to any company, industry, or situation. The rewrite names a concrete problem (outdated playbooks), creates tension (the gap), and implies a consequence (wasted budget). A reader recognizes themselves in it.

---

### Edit 2: Question Opener

**Category:** Opener
**Before:**
"Have you ever wondered why some email campaigns get 40% open rates while others struggle to break 10%? The answer might surprise you."

**After:**
"Our last product launch email got a 12% open rate. The apology email we sent the next week — the one where we admitted the launch was buggy — got 47%."

**Why it works:** The original asks a generic question and promises a surprise that never feels surprising. The rewrite uses a specific, real-seeming example that illustrates the answer (authenticity outperforms polish) without announcing it. The reader draws the conclusion themselves.

---

### Edit 3: Excited Announcement Opener

**Category:** Opener
**Before:**
"We are thrilled to announce the launch of our groundbreaking new platform that will revolutionize the way teams collaborate and drive productivity to new heights."

**After:**
"We built a thing. It lets your team see who is working on what without asking. Here is what it does and who it is for."

**Why it works:** The original is pure noise — "thrilled," "groundbreaking," "revolutionize," and "new heights" are all meaningless. The rewrite is blunt, specific, and respects the reader's time. It describes the actual capability in plain language.

## Transitions

### Edit 4: Formal Connector

**Category:** Transition
**Before:**
"Furthermore, it is worth noting that the implementation of these strategies can lead to significant improvements in customer retention metrics. Additionally, organizations that adopt this approach report higher levels of employee satisfaction."

**After:**
"Retention improved. But the part we did not expect: the support team stopped dreading Mondays. Fewer angry tickets. Fewer escalations. They actually liked the product they were supporting."

**Why it works:** "Furthermore" and "Additionally" are robotic connectors. The rewrite uses a natural shift — "But the part we did not expect" — that signals a genuine discovery, not a list item. The specific details (angry tickets, dreading Mondays) make it real.

---

### Edit 5: Contrast Transition

**Category:** Transition
**Before:**
"That being said, it is important to consider the potential challenges and limitations associated with this approach. On the other hand, the benefits often outweigh the drawbacks when implemented correctly."

**After:**
"This breaks in two situations: teams larger than 50 and anything with regulatory compliance requirements. If that is you, skip to the section on enterprise alternatives."

**Why it works:** The original hedges in both directions and says nothing. The rewrite names the exact limitations, tells the affected readers what to do, and moves on. Acknowledging weaknesses specifically builds more trust than vaguely mentioning "challenges."

## CTAs

### Edit 6: Generic CTA

**Category:** CTA
**Before:**
"Ready to take your marketing to the next level? Contact us today to learn more about how our comprehensive suite of solutions can help your business achieve its goals and drive meaningful growth."

**After:**
"We will audit your last 3 email campaigns and show you where the opens dropped. Free. Takes 20 minutes. Book a slot this week."

**Why it works:** "Take your marketing to the next level" means nothing. "Comprehensive suite of solutions" means nothing. The rewrite offers a specific deliverable (audit of 3 campaigns), a specific insight (where opens dropped), removes risk (free), sets expectations (20 minutes), and creates mild urgency (this week). The reader knows exactly what happens if they click.

---

### Edit 7: Newsletter CTA

**Category:** CTA
**Before:**
"Subscribe to our newsletter to stay up-to-date with the latest industry trends, expert insights, and actionable tips delivered straight to your inbox."

**After:**
"Every Tuesday, one idea that took us a week to figure out. No roundups. No sponsored content. Just the thing that actually worked. Join 4,200 marketers who read it."

**Why it works:** "Latest industry trends" and "expert insights" describe every newsletter ever. The rewrite is specific about frequency (Tuesday), format (one idea), quality bar (took a week to figure out), and what it excludes (no roundups, no sponsored). Social proof is concrete (4,200 marketers), not vague ("thousands of professionals").

## Adjectives

### Edit 8: Stacked Buzzwords

**Category:** Adjective
**Before:**
"Our innovative, cutting-edge platform provides a seamless, comprehensive solution for modern businesses looking to streamline their operations and achieve unprecedented results."

**After:**
"The platform connects your CRM, email tool, and analytics in one dashboard. Setup takes about 15 minutes. Most teams stop switching between tabs by day two."

**Why it works:** Five adjectives and not one piece of information. The rewrite replaces "innovative" with what it actually does (connects three tools), "seamless" with the experience (15-minute setup), and "unprecedented results" with a specific, believable outcome (stop switching tabs). The reader can evaluate whether this is useful to them.

---

### Edit 9: Robust and Scalable

**Category:** Adjective
**Before:**
"Our robust and scalable infrastructure ensures reliable performance even during peak usage periods, providing a best-in-class experience for enterprise customers."

**After:**
"We handled 2.3 million API calls during Shopify's Black Friday sale without a single timeout. The infrastructure scales automatically — no pre-provisioning, no support tickets."

**Why it works:** "Robust and scalable" is a claim. "2.3 million API calls with zero timeouts" is evidence. "Best-in-class" is an opinion with no source. "No pre-provisioning, no support tickets" describes what the customer actually experiences.

---

### Edit 10: Comprehensive Guide

**Category:** Adjective
**Before:**
"This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about content marketing strategy, from foundational concepts to advanced techniques that will help you achieve your goals."

**After:**
"This guide covers 6 things: how to find keywords worth targeting, how to write posts that rank, how to build links without begging, how to measure what is working, how to kill what is not, and how to do all of it with a 2-person team."

**Why it works:** "Comprehensive" and "everything you need to know" are unprovable claims. The rewrite lists exactly what is covered, so the reader can decide if it matches their needs. "Foundational to advanced" is replaced with specific topics. "Achieve your goals" is replaced with real constraints (2-person team).

## Filler

### Edit 11: Unnecessary Preface

**Category:** Filler
**Before:**
"It's important to note that not all analytics tools are created equal. When evaluating your options, it's worth mentioning that the key differentiator often lies in the quality of the data integrations and the ease of the reporting interface."

**After:**
"The difference between analytics tools comes down to two things: how cleanly they pull your data and how fast you can build a report. Everything else is feature bloat."

**Why it works:** Two filler phrases ("important to note," "worth mentioning") pad two sentences that say one thing. The rewrite states the thing directly and adds a point of view ("everything else is feature bloat") that the original was too cautious to express.

---

### Edit 12: Hedged Conclusion

**Category:** Filler
**Before:**
"At the end of the day, it could be argued that the most successful marketing teams are those that effectively balance data-driven decision making with creative intuition. Needless to say, finding this balance is an ongoing process that requires continuous refinement."

**After:**
"The best marketing teams trust numbers for what to do and instinct for how to do it. That balance never stabilizes. You recalibrate every quarter."

**Why it works:** "At the end of the day" and "needless to say" are pure filler. "It could be argued" is a hedge that weakens a reasonable claim. The rewrite makes the same point in half the words and adds a practical implication (recalibrate quarterly) that the reader can act on.

---

## How to Use This Library

1. When you encounter AI-sounding text, check if the pattern matches an entry in this library
2. If it matches, apply the transformation principle — do not copy the exact rewrite (that would trade one template for another)
3. If no entry matches, apply the underlying principle: replace abstract claims with specific evidence, replace announcements with actions, replace hedges with commitments
4. After applying edits, read the result aloud. If you would not say it in conversation with a smart colleague, rewrite again
